Fourteen sieges between 1309 and 1783, and today you walk in from Spain across an airport runway.
This 3D timelapse rebuilds Gibraltar from British and Spanish records and period maps: every date sourced, and where the two sides' numbers differ, we give both and say who counted.

Facts as of 18 October 2026. The UK–EU treaty on Gibraltar is in force only provisionally.

HOW THIS FILM WAS MADE
• Visuals: 3D reconstructions built from British and Spanish sources and period maps (Drinkwater's 1786 plan, Faden's 1783 plan, the 1705 plan, the 1943 US Army map), with terrain and today's town from open map data.
• Estimated (our reconstruction, not recorded): the 1782 ruins and the days they fall; siege-camp tents (placed from Faden's plan); everyday life on the isthmus (tents, fences, paths, carts, people, gardens, animals, low sheds), added as generic, unnamed detail; building heights; the 1782 coastline; borders.
• Troops, crowds and ships are illustrative in number and layout.
• Four short close-up scenes are AI-generated illustrations (Google Gemini): 0:04, 1:48, 3:27 and 4:01, 27 seconds in all. They add no facts; every fact is in the narration and the sources. Outside those four scenes nothing on screen is AI-generated, and buildings, street layout, flags, dates and numbers come from the sources.
• Narration is a synthetic voice (ElevenLabs).
• YouTube disclosure: AI use = Yes (set at upload), so YouTube may label this video as altered or synthetic.

NUMBERS, AND WHO COUNTED
• 14 sieges, 1309–1783: Sayer (1862) and the Gibraltar National Museum; García Sanz (2022) uses the same numbering.
• 4 August 1704 is the new-calendar date; English histories give 24 July.
• Rounds fired (Drinkwater): garrison 205,328 from September 1779; besiegers 258,387 from April 1781.
• Whole siege: Montero, about 2,000 defenders and 6,000 besiegers lost from all causes; the garrison's return (Drinkwater), 333 killed or died of wounds and 536 died of sickness.
