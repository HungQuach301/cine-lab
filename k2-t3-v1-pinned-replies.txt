--- Reply: "Fourteen sieges?"
Fourteen sieges? It depends on how you count. Sayer's History of Gibraltar (1862) and the Gibraltar National Museum count fourteen, from the Castilian capture of 1309 to the Great Siege of 1779–83; both 1333 sieges count, and the 1704 capture and the 1704–05 counter-siege count as two. A modern Spanish history (García Sanz, 2022) uses the same numbering: it calls the 1704 attack the eleventh siege. Some lists reach fifteen by adding the 1969–85 border closure; we kept to military sieges.

--- Reply: "Is this AI?"
Is this AI? Partly, and we say where. The narration is a synthetic voice. The 3D scenes are our own reconstructions, built from period maps and records. Four short close-up scenes (0:04, 1:48, 3:27, 4:01; 27 seconds in all) are AI-generated illustrations (Google Gemini), each started from a frame of our own 3D render; they add no facts. Outside those scenes nothing on screen is AI-generated, and AI supplied no dates, numbers, positions, flags or uniforms. Those come from the sources listed above.

--- Reply: "Why both sides' numbers?"
Why both sides' numbers? Because the two sides counted different things. For the floating batteries on 13–14 September 1782, a British estimate and the Spanish historian Montero both put attackers' losses above 2,000 (the British figure includes prisoners); a French account of 1785 counts 185 hit by gunfire before the fires; the garrison's own return lists 16 killed. For the whole siege, Montero gives about 2,000 defenders and 6,000 besiegers lost from all causes; the garrison's records count at least 869 dead. We show who counted, and leave both.

--- Reply: "Is the isthmus Spanish?"
Is the isthmus Spanish? The two governments disagree, and the film doesn't take a side. The 1713 Treaty of Utrecht (Art. X) names the town, castle, port and forts, not the sandy isthmus. Spain's foreign ministry says the isthmus was never ceded and does not recognise the fence as a border. In 2015 the UK's Europe minister told Parliament that Britain regards Gibraltar as including the isthmus. The 2026 UK–EU agreement says it changes neither side's position on sovereignty.
