There is an early signal about who feels it first. Brynjolfsson and his Stanford colleagues follow payroll records for millions of American workers. Through June 2026, they find no evidence of widespread job loss across the economy. But for workers aged twenty-two to twenty-five in the occupations most exposed to AI, employment now stands nineteen percent below where it would have been. Older workers show no such gap. And it happens mostly through fewer young people being hired, not more people being let go. Customer service representatives are among the largest jobs in the most exposed group. The authors call these early, descriptive signals, not proof of cause.
