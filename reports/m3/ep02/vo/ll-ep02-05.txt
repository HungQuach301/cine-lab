What happened to the women at the switchboards? Feigenbaum and Gross compared cities that were cut over with cities that were not. Ten years later, operators in cut-over cities were eight percentage points less likely to still be operators. Those over twenty-five were about seven points less likely to be working at all. Many of those still working changed occupations: about eleven percentage points more often than operators elsewhere. And many who found other work landed in lower-paid jobs. Their occupation scores fell five percent, while their peers' rose eight.
