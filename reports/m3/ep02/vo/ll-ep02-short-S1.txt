Between 1920 and 1940, the dial replaced telephone operators across America. What happened to them? Economists James Feigenbaum and Daniel Gross followed them in the census. Ten years later, operators in cut-over cities were eight percentage points less likely to still be operators. Older ones were about seven points less likely to be working at all. But the next generation of young women? Their overall employment did not fall. They found other work. Automation hurt the people in the job, not those who came after.
