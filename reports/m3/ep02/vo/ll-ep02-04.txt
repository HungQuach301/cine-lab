What changed was the switch. Between 1920 and 1940, AT&T replaced operators with mechanical switching in over half of the U.S. telephone network. Among Bell telephones, the share on dial service rose from thirty-two percent in 1930 to sixty percent in 1940. City by city, there was a night called the cutover. The switchboard went quiet, and the dial took over. Decades later, in 1965, Bell opened its first permanent fully electronic switching office, in Succasunna, New Jersey.
