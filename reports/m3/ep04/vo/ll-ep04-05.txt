The computer came for the office in two waves. In 1960, the BLS studied twenty offices that had just installed one. Of about two thousand eight hundred employees affected, only nine had been laid off a year later. Most were moved, or stayed put. The first machines went for the routine records: posting, checking, filing, computing. Only a small share of the affected workers did correspondence, stenographic, or secretarial work. The typing pool was spared, for a while. The second wave sat on every desk. By 1988, typists, word processors, and data entry keyers held about one point four million jobs, and the BLS wrote: with the proliferation of personal computers throughout the economy, more and more workers are performing work formerly done by typists.
