The lamplighter walks on. After the war, American officials worried about how little they knew of Soviet work, written in Russian. So Dostert, who had used machines to help interpreters, turned to a machine that would translate by itself. On January 7, 1954, his Georgetown team and IBM showed it in New York. The computer knew only two hundred fifty words and six rules of grammar, enough for those chosen sentences. The next morning it was on front pages, and the forecasts said a machine built just for translation could be ready within a few years.
