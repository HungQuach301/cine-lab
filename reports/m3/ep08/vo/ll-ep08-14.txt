Here is the comparison we can make, and it is a comparison of patterns, not of technologies. In both eras, a repetitive part of the work went to a machine, and people moved to the work around it. One difference matters. In 1966, the machine was not good enough, and the committee said so. This time, the machine is in everyday use, and the Oxford study finds it has slowed the growth of translator jobs. So what happened to the people who carry words between languages? When machine translation finally reached everyday use, the profession did not disappear. The work moved: from waiting for a speaker to stop, to speaking at the same time; from writing every sentence, to checking a machine's draft; from the courtroom booth, to the video call.
