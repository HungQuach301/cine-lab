1. MAIN MESSAGE: Machine translation was long promised and repeatedly overhyped, but when it arrived it did not erase translators; it moved their work (to checking drafts, remote and video interpreting). What stays human, for now, is the part where a wrong word matters.

2. TONE: (b). The closing section lists how the work moved ("from waiting for a speaker to stop... to a video call") and says people "did not disappear". Segments 11 and 14 add a caution that growth has slowed, so it is not pure reassurance.

3. HOOK: Partly. "What happened to the people who carry words from one language to another?" is answered by [15] "the people did not disappear. The work moved". But [14] leaves the future open ("how much of the work will stay with people, and which part?"), so it is not fully closed.

4. HARD TO FOLLOW:
- [01] The lamplighter and the library are not explained. "Tonight, it opens the story of another" is vague.
- [02] "That photograph" refers to nothing heard. A listener has no photo.
- [03] Four language channels, booths, warning light and thousands of documents arrive in one dense block. The point is only "simultaneous".
- [04] "In 1946... In 1949, he moved to Georgetown" is a date list with no payoff.
- [05] "Soviet work, published in Russian" is unclear. "Two hundred fifty words and six rules" is hard to grasp.
- [06] "Employed 262 translators and clerk-translators" and "four thousand under contract, used about three hundred" are hard to follow. Listeners may not see why this means "no shortage".
- [07] "Postediting" is introduced and then reused in [10] as "post-editing". "The word from 1966 is back" is easy to miss by ear.
- [10] Computer-assisted tools, localizers and machine translation software are three terms in one breath.
- [11] "Never created" jobs and "wages slowed then recovered 2016" are a lot to follow.
- [13] "Yet" is not heard in the handbook line, so [14] "The word yet in that sentence" points at something the listener cannot find.

5. BORING / TOO LONG: [03] (equipment detail), [06] (accounting), [10] (job-handbook list), [12] (list of demand drivers: "diverse population, globalization, military"), [13] (statistics).

6. NUMBERS:
- [05] "Three to five years" vs [15] "five years... more than fifty". These are loosely different claims.
- [06] 262 vs 4,000 vs 300 are three headcounts with unclear relation. $20M has no scale reference.
- [11] "28,000 positions never created" is an estimate, and it could be heard as 28,000 people losing jobs. It is flagged as an estimate, but that is easy to miss. "695 labor markets" is meaningless by ear.
- [13] 73,900 to 75,400 is hard to hear. The "2% vs 3.5%" comparison is for all jobs, not translators over the decade, and is easily misheard as "shrinking". "6,000 openings" is easily misread as new jobs when it is mostly replacement.
- [07] "Slightly longer" is fine. [08] "A third fewer errors" is fine, but one military agency is a thin base.

7. SOUNDS LIKE OPINION:
- [04] "It worked" is a judgement.
- [08] "The useful machine was the one that helped the person" is narrator framing.
- [14] "The pattern is familiar", "One difference matters", "good enough for everyday use" and the whole "yet" question are narrator interpretation.
- [15] "The people did not disappear", "What stays with people, for now, is the part where a wrong word matters" and [16] "Every era has its last lamplighters" are narrator's own conclusions, not sourced.

- [02] "That photograph" has no antecedent.
- [04] to [05] is a lamplighter line dropped in with no meaning.
- [08] to [09] to [10] moves from 1966 to a present-day screen with no year bridge. "After 2010" follows abruptly.

9. TOP 3 FIXES:
1. Cut and simplify the numbers in [05], [06], [11] and [13] to one or two memorable figures each. State plainly that 28,000 is "fewer jobs created, not people fired" and that 73,900 to 75,400 is slow growth, not decline.
2. Trim [03], [04], [10] and [12] detail, and fix signposting: remove "That photograph", say what the lamplighter stands for early, and bridge [08] to [10] with a date.
3. Attribute the judgements in [04] and [14] to sources, or mark them as the narrator's view. Make "yet" audible in [13] or drop the pointer in [14].
