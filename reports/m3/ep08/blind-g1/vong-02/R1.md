1. MAIN MESSAGE: Machine translation was forecast in 1954 to arrive within a few years but took over 50 years. When it came, the translation profession did not vanish; the work shifted (to post-editing, remote and video interpreting), though machines have slowed job growth.

2. TONE: (b). It repeatedly frames change as a shift in tasks ("the work moved"; "Slower growth, not decline"; "the flame keeps changing hands"). It does admit a real cost (the Oxford finding), so it is not pure reassurance.

3. HOOK: Partly. The question is what happened to the people who carry words between languages. The closest answer is [15] "the profession did not disappear. The work moved...". But [15] closes on a new open question ("which part of the work stays with people, and for how long?"), and the [14] contrast with "slowed the growth" muddies it.
