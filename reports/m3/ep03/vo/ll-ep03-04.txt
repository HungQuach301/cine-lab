Why not? Because the machine made each branch cheaper to run. Between 1988 and 2004, the number of tellers needed to operate an average urban branch fell from twenty to thirteen. So banks did something unexpected. They opened more branches. In urban areas, Bessen reports, forty-three percent more. Fewer tellers per branch, but many more branches. Bessen puts the logic simply: the ATM let banks run branch offices at lower cost, and that prompted them to open many more of them. A machine that saved labor at each window created demand for more windows.
