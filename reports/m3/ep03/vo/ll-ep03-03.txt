Then came a machine built for the most repetitive part of the job. Cash machines were first installed in the United States in the 1970s. A machine could hand out cash, take a deposit, and report a balance, at any hour, without a person at the window. From the mid-1990s, banks rolled them out fast. By 2015, economist James Bessen counted more than four hundred thousand of them in the United States alone. You might expect the tellers to vanish. They did not.
