Does the software actually judge credit better? Economists at the Federal Reserve Bank of Philadelphia tested a machine-learning model on U.S. credit bureau records. In every year from 2004 to 2019, it predicted defaults better than a traditional statistical model. Under their assumptions, the difference was worth about two percent in profit. It is a test of models, not a count of jobs. But it shows why banks are moving the scoring to machines.
