When cash machines spread across America, everyone expected bank tellers to disappear. They did not. Economist James Bessen found that between 1988 and 2004, an average urban branch needed fewer tellers, twenty down to thirteen. But branches got cheaper to run, so banks opened more of them. Fewer tellers per branch, more branches, and a new job: talking to customers instead of counting cash.
