For most of the twentieth century, banking meant a person at a window. The teller counted your cash, checked your signature, and wrote the deposit in your passbook. It was careful, repetitive work, and after the Second World War there was more and more of it. In 1950, the U.S. census counted about sixty-five thousand bank tellers. By 1970, on a newer census basis, about two hundred fifty-three thousand. That growth came before cash machines, in the long postwar boom.
