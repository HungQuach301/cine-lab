For most of the twentieth century, banking meant a person at a window. The teller counted your cash, checked your signature, and wrote the deposit in your passbook. It was careful, repetitive work, and after the Second World War there was more and more of it. In 1950, the U.S. census counted about sixty-five thousand bank tellers. By 1960, about twice as many. The census then changed how it sorted jobs, so the next decade is a separate line: about one hundred thirty-five thousand bank tellers in 1960, and about two hundred fifty-three thousand by 1970. That is up about eighty-seven percent in ten years.
