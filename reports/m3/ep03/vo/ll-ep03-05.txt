And the job itself changed. Counting cash mattered less. Talking to customers mattered more. In Bessen's words, tellers became an important part of the relationship banking team: selling accounts, explaining loans, solving problems. In census and survey data, full-time-equivalent teller employment kept rising after 2000, by about two percent a year, faster than the labor force as a whole. Bessen's point is not that machines never cost jobs. It is that when a machine takes one task, the job can survive if people move to the tasks that are left. The machine did not end the job. It changed what the job was for.
