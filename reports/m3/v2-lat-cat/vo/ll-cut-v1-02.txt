The typewriter made a new kind of job almost overnight. Letters, contracts, invoices, all of it now had to be typed, and someone had to take it down first in shorthand. In 1900, the U.S. census counted about one hundred thirty-four thousand stenographers, typists, and secretaries. By 1930, about one point one million. By 1950, about one point seven million. The census itself warns that the 1900 figures are approximations, and that definitions shifted from count to count. So read the early line as a shape, not a precise measure. And the shape is a boom.
