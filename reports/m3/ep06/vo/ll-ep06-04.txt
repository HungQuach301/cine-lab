By 1988, composing room workers held about eighty-six thousand jobs in the United States, more than a third of them at newspapers. The BLS expected little or no change in their numbers through the year 2000, despite a growing printing industry, because computers were taking over much of the typing. The training changed too. In the past, almost all compositors learned through an apprenticeship. By 1990, new technologies had lowered skill requirements, and most learned the craft on the job. At newspapers, it was blunt: equipment that lets reporters and editors format pages at a desktop terminal has already eliminated many typesetting and composition jobs, and more are certain to disappear.
