But the work did not vanish. It moved. The economist James Bessen describes how it happened. Designers used to draw a page and send it to typesetters, then mark revisions and send it back. With desktop publishing, it became easier for designers to use the software themselves. Designers using computers substituted for typesetters. In his words: there are fewer typesetters, but more graphic designers and desktop publishers. And the designers had to learn entirely new skills to use the new technology. In 1988, visual artists held about two hundred sixteen thousand jobs, and the BLS expected them to grow faster than average, though computers would limit that growth.
