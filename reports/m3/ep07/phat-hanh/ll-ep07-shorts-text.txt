S1 — 'Computer' used to be a job title
Before electronic computers, the word computer meant a person: someone who did mathematical calculations by hand. In 1935, five women formed the first computer pool at the Langley aeronautical laboratory in Virginia. Full film: "When Computers Were People: One Word, Three Jobs" on the channel.
Source: NASA History, "When the Computer Wore a Skirt: Langley's Computers, 1935–1970". #history #jobs #computers

S2 — Programmers vs. developers, 2025–35
Two job titles that sound alike are heading in different directions. BLS projects computer programmers to shrink from 2025 to 2035, while software developers are projected to grow from 1.72 million to 1.89 million by 2035. Projections, not counts. Full film on the channel.
Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook (projections 2025–35). #jobs #software #AI

S3 — 1990: AI as 'just additional tools'
In 1990, the government's job handbook looked ahead: improved software would simplify or eliminate some programming tasks, and AI principles and languages would become "just additional tools available to programmers". Full film on the channel.
Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook 1990–91. #history #programming #AI
