In the 1960s, the job on its way out was the elevator operator. Today, the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics has a list of its own: the jobs it projects will shrink fastest between 2025 and 2035.
At the top, word processors and typists: down thirty-four percent. Telephone operators, down twenty-eight. Switchboard operators and data entry keyers, down about a quarter. Telemarketers, down a fifth.
Not the same technology. The same pattern: one task, repeated, and a machine that can repeat it.
The Bureau names AI in its forecasts. In its 2023 to 2033 projections, it expected AI to mostly affect jobs whose core tasks generative AI can most easily copy. Customer service representatives: down five percent. Auto damage appraisers: down nine. Claims adjusters: down about four.
Some jobs grow. In that same round, software developers: up almost eighteen percent. In the newest projections, for 2025 to 2035: nurse practitioners up forty-one, solar installers up more than thirty-six, data scientists up about thirty-five.
Some jobs end. Some change. Some are new.
