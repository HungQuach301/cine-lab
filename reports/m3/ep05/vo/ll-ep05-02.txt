Behind every claim, there used to be a small army of clerks. In the 1950s, insurance was paperwork on an enormous scale: premiums to bill, policies to update, claims to record and pay. In 1964, U.S. insurance carriers employed about eight hundred eighty-five thousand people, and most of them did office work. Then a new machine arrived at head office: the electronic computer.
