So here is the comparison, with a caution. Not the same job: the same claims desk. We will not set these numbers side by side. One is a survey of office workers over nine years; the other is a forecast for a single occupation. What we can compare is the line. In 1966, the BLS drew it at judgment and decision making: the computer kept the records, and people decided what a claim was worth. Today, the BLS says software can evaluate photographs of damaged property and calculate an estimated claim amount. Not the same technology. The same pattern: a repetitive human task, taken over by an automated system. But the differences matter. Back then, the machine kept the records. Now software drafts the first estimate, the step that once needed a person's eye. The final call, so far, still sits with people.
