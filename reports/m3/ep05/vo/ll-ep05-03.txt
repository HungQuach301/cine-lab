In 1966, the Bureau of Labor Statistics published a study of what the computer was doing to insurance offices. Employment in company computer units soared, from about six hundred people in 1954 to about sixteen thousand by 1963. And the routine work began to move. Routine clerical record keeping declined in two-thirds of the companies surveyed. Jobs like premium billing, tasks which formerly required large numbers of clerks handling files, went to the machine. So did the policy evaluation clerks, who calculated the cash values of policies using rate books and manual calculators.
