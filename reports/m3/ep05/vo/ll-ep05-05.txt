And office jobs did not collapse. In two hundred seven companies surveyed, office employment reached about three hundred sixty-six thousand by January 1963, up thirty-one point five percent over nine years, though it grew more slowly than before. Few people were laid off as a direct result. The BLS pointed to high turnover among young clerical workers, a growing industry, and some new computer jobs. And it drew a line: jobs requiring some judgment and decision making, jobs that cannot be computerized, would remain.
