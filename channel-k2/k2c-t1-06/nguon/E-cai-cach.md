## 9. 2025–2026 STATE LAWS (most relevant)

**E-TX-1 [OFFICIAL] Texas SB 840 (89th Leg., 2025), enrolled text** https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB00840F.htm [tx_sb840_enr.htm]; history [tx_sb840_history.html]: "Signed by the Governor"; "Effective on 9/1/25".
- Applies to "a municipality with a population greater than 150,000 that is wholly or partly located in a county with a population greater than 300,000" (text).
- "mixed-use residential" = residential "at least 65 percent of the total square footage"; "multifamily residential" = "three or more dwelling units".
- City may not impose "a limit on density that is more restrictive than the greater of: (i) the highest residential density allowed in the municipality; or (ii) 36 units per acre; (B) a limit on building height that is more restrictive than the greater of: (i) the highest height that would apply to an office, commercial, retail, or warehouse development constructed on the site; or (ii) 45 feet"; also parking limited (no "more than one parking space per dwelling" unit).
- House committee bill analysis (C.S.S.B. 840, By: Hughes) [tx_sb840_bill_analysis.htm]: "requires a municipality to allow mixed-use residential use and development or multifamily residential use and development in a zoning classification that allows office, commercial, retail, warehouse, or mixed-use use"; background cites comptroller: Texas "more than 300,000 homes short ... as of 2021".
**E-TX-2 [OFFICIAL] Texas SB 15 (2025), enrolled** https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/html/SB00015F.htm [tx_sb15_enr.htm]; "Effective on 9/1/25".
- Sec. 211.054: "A municipality may not adopt or enforce an ordinance, rule, or other measure that requires: (1) a residential lot to be: (A) larger than 3,000 square feet; (B) wider than 30 feet; or (C) deeper than 75 feet" (applies to cities >150,000 in counties >300,000, for subdivisions/lots of "five acres or more").
- Note: SB 15 is small-lot single-family, NOT apartments — relevant only as context.
**E-MA-1 [OFFICIAL] M.G.L. c.40A §3A (MBTA Communities Act)** https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleVII/Chapter40A/Section3A [ma_40A_3A.html]
- "An MBTA community shall have a zoning ordinance or by-law that provides for at least 1 district of reasonable size in which multi-family housing is permitted as of right ... (i) have a minimum gross density of 15 units per acre ... (ii) be located not more than 0.5 miles from a commuter rail station, subway station, ferry terminal or bus station"
- SJC: Attorney General v. Town of Milton, SJC-13580 — read via Town of Sutton-hosted summary [ma_sjc_milton.pdf]: Court held "(1) the Act is constitutional ... (2) the Attorney General has the power to enforce the Act ... (3) the 'guidelines' issued by EOHLC constitute a 'regulation' ... renders the current Guidelines unenforceable." **Date caution:** the summary says "January 8, 2024" but also says Milton voters rejected rezoning "In February of 2024" — so the decision date is Jan 8, **2025** (summary typo; mass.gov and Justia both 403 — official opinion not read).
