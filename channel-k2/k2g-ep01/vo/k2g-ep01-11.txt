Then there's a route that skips coke entirely. In August 2021, the Swedish steelmaker SSAB announced it had delivered the world's first steel made with iron reduced by fossil-free hydrogen instead of coal and coke: a trial delivery to the Volvo Group. You'll hear two opposite claims: that green steel is already here, and that it's only marketing. The record sits in between: a trial delivery is real steel, and the record shows the route has produced it; on its own, it doesn't show an industry at scale. A company called Stegra is building a hydrogen-based steel plant in Boden, in northern Sweden. In June 2026 it closed a one point four billion euro financing round and said its project timeline was "under review." As of October 11, 2026, the most recent reports we found: on September 30, Tech.eu and Finwire reported that Stegra's cost to complete the plant was "significantly higher than assumed in June" and that it needs more capital; Finwire reported that the timeline for green steel "remains unchanged." We won't guess when it opens. Our read: a route built on hydrogen depends on new inputs, such as fossil-free hydrogen, so where those are produced becomes part of the path again — as coke and ore once were in Pittsburgh.
