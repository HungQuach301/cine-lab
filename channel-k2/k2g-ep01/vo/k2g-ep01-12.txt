So here is our read of the mechanism under all three layers. In the 1880s, power in steel meant controlling the cheapest path from rock to rail: ore, coke, limestone, railroads, river. Carnegie's firm secured a key piece of it: the coke. Since 2018, Washington — and, with its own measures, Brussels — have put hard limits on steel imports; as of October 2026, Washington treats it as a security question. Tariffs and quotas set terms for who sells steel across a border; a golden share sets terms for some decisions inside one company. A carbon price is a different tool. It doesn't own anything. It's a toll — and a toll changes which paths are cheapest. One question: who controls the path, and who sets the toll?
