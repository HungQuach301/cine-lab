In March 1901, Carnegie wrote, the banker J. P. Morgan approached him through Charles Schwab and asked whether he really wished to retire from business. Carnegie answered yes. A group led by Morgan and Elbert Gary bought Carnegie's company and combined it with the Federal Steel Company. The result was United States Steel — "born in 1901," as the company's history puts it — the company at the center of layer two. According to the U.S. Geological Survey, the United States made about nine million tonnes of steel in 1900. In 1944, it made about eighty-one million — roughly half of all the steel made on Earth that year. In 1950, still close to half. Our read: that's "steel as national power" in numbers — a share. One explanation for the 1950 figure: much of the world was still rebuilding after the war — an unusual moment, not a baseline.
