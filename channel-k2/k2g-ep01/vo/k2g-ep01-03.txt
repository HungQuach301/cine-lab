This is the first piece of the mechanism. Years after those first rails, in his autobiography, he wrote down the whole chain behind a single ton of steel. One and a half tons of iron ore: mined, carried by rail about a hundred miles to the Great Lakes, then by boat for hundreds of miles, then by rail again, a hundred and fifty miles, to Pittsburgh. One and a half tons of coal, turned into coke and carried fifty-odd miles. And a ton of limestone, carried a hundred and fifty miles. Four tons of material, moved hundreds of miles, to make one ton of steel. Writing about the late 1880s and 1890s, Carnegie said steel was being sold at "three pounds for two cents." Our read: it's geography plus logistics. Whoever controls the cheapest path from rock to rail gains an edge that everyone else has to answer.
