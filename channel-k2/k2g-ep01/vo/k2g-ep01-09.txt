And then the question reached the company Morgan's group had formed. In December 2023, U.S. Steel announced it would be acquired by Japan's Nippon Steel. On January 3, 2025, President Biden issued an order prohibiting the acquisition. President Trump then ordered a new CFIUS review, and on June 13, 2025, he issued an order allowing the acquisition, on the condition of a national security agreement. Five days later, the deal closed. As part of the deal, U.S. Steel issued the U.S. government a "golden share," the companies said. A second popular claim worth checking: that the golden share means Washington now owns U.S. Steel. The companies' own announcement describes something narrower. The share lets the government appoint one independent director, and gives the president consent rights over specific decisions, including cutting investment commitments in the agreement, changing the name or headquarters, moving production or jobs abroad, and certain plant closures or idlings. The full agreement has not been made public. Three months later, the White House said it had used the threat of that power. U.S. Steel had said it would stop processing steel slabs at its Granite City plant in Illinois. According to the Associated Press on September 22, 2025, the White House said it intervened "with the threat of the president invoking his golden share authority."
