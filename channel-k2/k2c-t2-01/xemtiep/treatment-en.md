# Treatment (what you will see) — "Shipbuilding", about 11 minutes

**Look.** Animated 3D relief maps (real terrain, seen from a low, gliding camera), in two palettes: old sepia paper for the past (1920s–1970s) and natural colours for today. Small 3D objects with a white sticker outline stand *inside* the map and cast real shadows: cargo ships, gantry cranes, coins, paper order slips. Real archival material for 10–15 % of the time: 1940s government photos and newsreels (shown in a bordered frame when the film is low-resolution), government documents with highlighted lines, a 1943 photo of a real welder. No presenter, no AI faces.

**The story is told through three coastlines, filmed the same way:** San Francisco Bay (Richmond, 1940s), Korea's south-east coast (Ulsan, 1970s) and the Delaware River (Philadelphia, today).

**Recurring motif — "the order comes before the shipyard":** a cream paper order slip with a red stamp drops onto the map *before* any crane or ship appears. 1940: an order from Britain lands on Richmond, then cranes rise and ships slide into the water. 1944: the last slip fades and the cranes stand still. 1972: an order from a Greek shipowner lands on an empty Korean beach, then cranes, docks and two giant tankers grow out of it while the map turns from sepia to full colour. 2026: a Pentagon order lands on Philadelphia, filmed at the same angle as Korea.

**Emotional curve by chapter.**
1. Cold open (0:00–0:54): three ships launching in a row in sepia; a column for China's share rising next to a near-flat U.S. column; a highlighted government notice and a countdown to November 9.
2. Richmond (0:54–2:09) — pride and speed: on the bay, three 3D columns show days to build one ship: 122 → 39 → a sliver for the 4½-day stunt; the welder's photo comes alive in 2.5D.
3. The law (2:09–3:48) — things slow down: the order slip fades, cranes freeze; a split screen shows supporters and critics of the Jones Act, each with their own quote.
4. The wages myth (3:48–5:13) — the "aha": thick 3D coins drop on the coast, 1 "world price" coin next to 4 "U.S.-built" coins; then a glowing dashed loop links coins → a thin order slip → a frozen crane: "High prices mean few orders. Few orders mean no scale. No scale keeps prices high."
5. Ulsan (5:13–7:26) — the underdog rises: the camera dives from the whole Korean peninsula to one empty beach; the order slip lands first; the yard grows; sepia turns to colour and dissolves into a real photo of the shipyard today.
6. Today (7:26–9:53) — tension, both sides: five unions' petition highlighted; China's share spreading across the map; a split screen of the U.S. finding and China's response; the fee and its suspension; Philadelphia, where the order slip lands again.
7. Ending (9:53–10:57) — calm and clear: three things to watch pinned on the map; a scoreboard (1943 peak vs today); the camera returns to Richmond, now a national park.

**Three key moments:** the shrinking day-columns at Richmond; the coin loop; the order slip landing on the empty beach in Ulsan.
