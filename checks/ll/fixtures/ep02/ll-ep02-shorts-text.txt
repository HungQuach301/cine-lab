S1 — Automation hurt the operators — not the next generation
Between 1920 and 1940 the dial replaced telephone operators across America. Economists followed them in the census: the women already at the switchboards paid the price; the next generation found other work. Full film: "Automation Hurt the Phone Operators. Not the Next Generation. What About Us?" on the channel.
Source: Feigenbaum & Gross, NBER Working Paper 28061. #history #jobs #automation

S2 — How many U.S. customer service jobs could disappear?
About 2.7 million Americans work in customer service. BLS projects about −5% by 2035 (2,666,000 → 2,524,100). A projection, not a count. Full film on the channel.
Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook (2025–35). #jobs #AI #customerservice

S3 — The night the switchboard went quiet
City by city, the "cutover" replaced operators with the dial. Bell telephones on dial service: 32% in 1930, 60% in 1940. Full film on the channel.
Source: Feigenbaum & Gross, NBER Working Paper 28061. #history #telephone
